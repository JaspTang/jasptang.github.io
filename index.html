02 / First model

I know Java, I just learnt Newton's Laws - can I model gravity using code?

In eighth grade, I thought it was so cool you could describe gravity using Newton's laws. How much cooler, I thought, would it be if I could personally simulate it using code... so I built a physics simulation involving gravity, momentum, friction, energy loss.

It was a small attempt to understand why things move the way they do. And more importantly it was the first time I felt the particular thrill of turning a question I had into a system I could touch and experiment with.

03 / A different scale

Computers are able to perform trillions of calculations a second. How does that work?

Jumping to the present day, my curiosity has led me way further down the rabbit hole... One question has always hit me from childhood: how are a whole bunch of 0's and 1's able to create lifelike games seemingly updating at 100 times a second. With my new learnt knowledge in computer architecture and digital logic design, I could finalyl confront this question.

I spent my time learning about GPU architecture while experimenting with CUDA to model some heat-related differential equations. It will never not be cool to me how I can program a GPU to run millions of operations at the same time. For this reason, I am now diving into building my own barebones GPU!

04 / Right now

Now, what am I up to?

It may seem like we know everything, but I love that we don't. Computing is still evolving, and every new architecture, accelerator, and manufacturing breakthrough opens another set of questions.

I started with something as simple as wondering why my computer crashed while playing Minecraft. Since then, I have kept asking what lies underneath each layer of computation. Now, I want to really understand how we can take the laws of physics, turn them into transistors, turn those transistors into logic, and ultimately turn a piece of silicon into a machine capable of computation - a machine only made possible through generations of human curiosity.
