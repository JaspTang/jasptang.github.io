PROJECT / ORIGIN EXPERIMENT

A small world, with rules you can touch.

In eighth grade, I built a bouncing-ball physics simulation to see gravity, momentum, friction, and energy loss play out together. It was an early way to turn a question about the world into a model I could experiment with.

Inspiration

After learning F=ma, I was amazed that a few rules could describe the motion I saw around me. I wanted to make those rules visible and find out what happened when objects bounced, slowed down, and collided.

Process

The model updates each ball's position and velocity over time, applies gravity, and responds to the edges with bouncing and energy loss. Drag on the stage to launch a new ball and watch the rules interact.

It was a small program, but it taught me how a system can emerge from simple updates repeated over time.

Results

Changing a rule and watching the result helped me understand the system in a way a description alone could not. That impulse to build a model and learn from it still guides the projects I choose.
